Quick start for new players

D&D is collaborative storytelling: you play a character while a Dungeon Master presents scenes and challenges. This app builds a complete sheet for that character, step by step.

Easy first hero: Race Human or Half-Elf, class Fighter or Cleric, ability scores Standard Array. Green Recommended badges mark newcomer-friendly options.

Edition: Header toggle 5e (2014) vs 5.5e (2024). If your DM did not specify, 2014 5e is still the common default.

1. Create a character

From the roster, New Character opens a ten-step wizard (name through summary). On equipment, Load Starter Kit can pre-fill gear. Expand New to D&D? on any step for a short explanation.

2. Rolling Ability Scores

On step 6 (Ability Scores), click Roll 4d6 (Drop Lowest). The app rolls four six-sided dice for each stat, drops the lowest die (shown crossed out in red), and totals the remaining three. You then assign each total to the stat of your choice. You can also type scores manually.

3. Playing Your Character

Click Play on a character card to open the session view. The Character tab shows your sheet — ability checks, saving throws, skills, senses, and features. Click any Roll button to roll a d20 with the correct modifier.

4. Combat

The Character tab also has a combat section: track HP, temporary HP, hit dice, death saves, conditions, exhaustion, and inspiration. Use the quick buttons (−1, −5, +1, +5) for fast HP changes. Short Rest resets some resources; Long Rest restores HP, half your hit dice, and all spell slots.

5. Spells & Actions

The Actions & Spells tab lets you add weapons (with attack/damage rolls), track spell slots, and manage known spells. Click a spell name to see its description. Click Cast to use a slot and optionally auto-roll damage/healing. Toggle the checkboxes to control auto-roll behavior.

6. Inventory

The Inventory tab tracks your currency (PP, GP, EP, SP, CP) and backpack items. Add items from the dropdown or type custom ones.

7. Leveling Up

The Leveling tab supports XP or Milestone mode. Add XP or click Level Up. At ASI levels (4, 8, 12, 16, 19 — plus 6 and 14 for Fighter, 10 for Rogue), you pick ability score improvements (+2 to one or +1 to two). Some subclasses also grant bonus proficiencies at certain levels.

8. DM Mode

Click DM in the header to switch to Dungeon Master mode. Browse and search monsters, create NPCs, and manage a grid-based battle map with tokens and terrain.

9. Battle Map

Open the battle map from the header or session view. Set grid size, add tokens, drag them between cells, and paint terrain (difficult, walls, elevation). Save maps for reuse.

10. Customization

Use Rules & Reference to add house rules, campaign notes, and toggle rule books. See the Guides tab for adding custom races, classes, spells, and content books.

Adding a monster

Adding a spell

Edit js/data/spells.js → SPELLS. Include: name, level (0=cantrip), school, castTime, range, components, duration, description, classes.

Edit js/data/data.js → ADDITIONAL_BOOKS. Add { id, name, enabled, races, classes, backgrounds, rules }.

Quick Rules Reference

AC = 10 + Dex (unarmored), or armor base + Dex cap + shield
Initiative = Dexterity modifier
Proficiency = +2 at 1st–4th level, +3 at 5th–8th, +4 at 9th–12th, +5 at 13th+
Passive Perception = 10 + Perception modifier
Advantage = roll 2d20, take higher
Disadvantage = roll 2d20, take lower
Critical hit = double dice, not modifiers
Short rest = 1 hour; spend Hit Dice to heal
Long rest = 8 hours; restore HP, half Hit Dice
Death saves = d20, 10+ success, 9− fail; 3 successes stabilize, 3 failures die

    Choose your race

    Your race determines your ancestry and grants special traits.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: Race = your ancestry (human, elf, dwarf, etc.) with unique traits and stat bonuses. Your race (or species) is what kind of being you are. Each race has unique abilities called "traits" and bonuses to certain stats. New players: Human, Half-Elf, and Hill Dwarf are great versatile choices. Look for the "Recommended" badge!

    3

    Choose your subrace

    Subraces offer additional traits and ability score increases.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: Subraces are variations within your race (e.g., High Elf vs Wood Elf). Most races have subraces — variations within the same species. For example, elves can be High Elves (smart, magic), Wood Elves (fast, stealthy), or Drow (dark, underground). Each subrace gives different stat bonuses and abilities. Pick the one that sounds most fun to you!

    4

    Choose your class

    Your class defines your primary abilities. Set starting level first—it affects subclass options and multiclassing.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: Class = your main role (Fighter, Wizard, Cleric, etc.) — it defines how you fight and what you can do. Your class is the most important choice — it determines your playstyle. Fighter = tough melee warrior, Wizard = powerful spellcaster, Rogue = stealthy skill expert, Cleric = healer and divine magic. Most new players start at level 1. Look for the "Recommended" badge for beginner-friendly classes!

    Choose your subclass

    Subclasses specialize your class. If your level is too low for any subclass, you can skip.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: Subclass = your specialization within your class (e.g., Champion vs Battle Master for Fighters). A subclass is your specialization within your class. For example, a Fighter can become a Champion (simple & strong), Battle Master (tactical maneuvers), or Eldritch Knight (fighter + magic). Most classes unlock subclasses at level 3 — if you're level 1, you'll pick this later when you level up.

    Add additional classes at level 2+. Total level = sum of all class levels (max 20). Requires level 2 or higher.

    6

    Set your ability scores

    Roll, use Standard Array, Point Buy, or enter manually. Race bonuses apply automatically.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: The 6 core numbers (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA) that define your character's strengths. Ability scores are the 6 core stats that define your character:
    Strength — physical power, melee attacks, carrying things
    Dexterity — agility, ranged attacks, stealth, AC
    Constitution — toughness, hit points, endurance
    Intelligence — knowledge, investigation, wizard spells
    Wisdom — perception, insight, cleric/druid spells
    Charisma — personality, persuasion, bard/sorcerer/warlock spells

    New players: use "Standard Array" for balanced scores, or "Point Buy" for more control. Put your highest score in your class's primary ability!

    Roll, use the standard array (15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8), point buy (27 points), or type scores manually.

    Choose your alignment

    Alignment describes your character's moral and ethical outlook.

    New to D&D? What's this?

    Summary: A 2-axis compass (Good/Evil × Lawful/Chaotic) for roleplay — most heroes are Good. Alignment is a simple 2-axis compass for your character's personality: Good/Neutral/Evil (morality) and Lawful/Neutral/Chaotic (attitude toward rules). Most heroes are Good. "Chaotic Good" = does the right thing their own way. "Lawful Good" = follows rules and does good. Don't overthink it — it's just a guide for roleplay!
